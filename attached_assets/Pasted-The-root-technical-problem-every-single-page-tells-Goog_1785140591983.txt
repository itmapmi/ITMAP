The root technical problem: every single page tells Google it's a duplicate of the homepage. I checked the raw HTML that crawlers initially receive (before any JavaScript runs) for the homepage, /platform, /pricing, /about, /licensing, and several /markets/ pages. Every one of them ships the identical canonical tag (<link rel="canonical" href="https://itmap.ai/">) and the identical <title> and meta description, all hardcoded to the homepage's values. Your React app does update these client-side with JavaScript after it loads (e.g., /markets correctly shows a distinct title once rendered), but the raw source Google and other crawlers see first is identical across all pages. A canonical tag pointing every URL back to the homepage is effectively telling Google "don't index this separately, it's the same as the homepage" — which lines up exactly with what your Search Console shows: only 2 of your ~30 sitemap URLs are indexed, and one page is stuck at "Crawled – currently not indexed."
Second major issue: the site is a fully client-rendered app with no server-side rendering. The raw HTML body is just <div id="root"></div> plus a one-sentence noscript fallback — none of your actual content (market names, vendor scores, headings) exists until JavaScript executes. Google can eventually render JS, but it's a slower, second-pass process for a new/low-authority domain, and crawlers that don't execute JavaScript at all (Bing, LinkedIn/X preview cards, and notably the AI crawlers you explicitly welcome in robots.txt — GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot) will see nothing but that placeholder sentence.
Third issue: your market detail pages are thin/gated for anonymous visitors. I checked /markets/object_storage_platforms as a logged-out user — instead of real content, it just shows "This market research is available to registered users. Sign in or create a free account..." These pages are exactly the ones best positioned to rank for valuable long-tail B2B searches (e.g., "backup target appliances vendors," "object storage platforms comparison"), but there's no substantive, unique text for Google to index or match to those queries.
Fourth: your sitemap isn't submitted in Search Console. You have a clean, well-structured sitemap.xml with ~30 URLs and correct lastmod/priority values, but the Sitemaps report in GSC shows zero submitted sitemaps. This is a 30-second fix and gives Google a direct discovery path.
On the positive side, your fundamentals are otherwise solid: robots.txt is well-configured and permissive, you have Organization/WebSite JSON-LD structured data, and your homepage meta description and OG tags are well-written.
Given all that, here's my prioritized fix list: first, submit sitemap.xml in Search Console immediately (trivial, do it today). Second and most urgent, fix the canonical tag so each page emits its own self-referencing canonical in the raw server response rather than a hardcoded homepage URL — this is likely single-handedly suppressing indexing of ~28 pages. Third, move to server-side rendering or static generation (Next.js, or a prerendering service) so unique titles, meta descriptions, and full content are present in the initial HTML for every route, not just after client-side JS runs. Fourth, give logged-out visitors and crawlers a substantive public preview on each market page (vendor names, a written market overview, a few sample scores/FAQs) rather than an immediate login wall, saving deeper proprietary data for registered users. Fifth, once that's fixed, invest in off-page signals like directory listings (G2, Capterra), industry PR, and backlinks, since right now you're only getting impressions for your own brand name with essentially no non-branded search visibility to build on.
